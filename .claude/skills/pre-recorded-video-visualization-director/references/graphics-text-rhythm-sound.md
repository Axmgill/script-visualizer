## Phase 10 — Motion Graphics Plan

Plan where graphics support meaning, not decoration.

Do this:

- Identify moments that need emphasis.
- Identify moments that need diagrams, arrows, highlights, counters, labels, callouts, logos, lower thirds, or animated shapes.
- Match each graphic to a spoken word or idea.
- Avoid graphics that distract from the voice.
- Keep motion aligned with editing rhythm.

## Phase 11 — Caption and Text Strategy

Plan subtitles, keywords, and emphasis text for the pre-recorded speech.

Do this:

- Decide whether captions are full, partial, word-by-word, phrase-by-phrase, or selective.
- Identify power words that should appear large.
- Identify words that should animate in sync with speech.
- Keep text inside platform safe zones.
- Avoid blocking faces, logos, or key action.
- Make text readable on mobile.

Emphasis examples:

- "fast" → punch-in text
- "real" → underline or highlight
- "you" → direct address emphasis
- "now" → kinetic typography

## Phase 12 — Editing Rhythm Plan

Create the cut map for the pre-recorded video.

Do this:

- Mark where cuts should happen.
- Mark where cuts should land on words, breaths, beats, pauses, or visual actions.
- Identify quick-cut sections.
- Identify slower hold sections.
- Identify moments for jump cuts, match cuts, whip transitions, zoom punches, freeze frames, silent pauses, speed ramps, or L-cuts/J-cuts.
- Keep rhythm connected to emotion and speech.

Also include: hook timing; retention spikes; pattern interrupts; breathing moments; final payoff timing; where to remove dead air; where to preserve natural pause for impact.

## Phase 13 — Sound and Music Direction

Plan audio even though the main focus is visualization.

Do this:

- Identify music mood.
- Identify tempo range.
- Identify where the beat drops or changes.
- Identify sound effects.
- Identify voiceover treatment.
- Identify moments where music should cut out for emphasis.
- Identify ambient sound or room tone.
- Sync audio events to transcript timecodes.
